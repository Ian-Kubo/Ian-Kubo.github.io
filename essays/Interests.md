## Life is a Journey, Not a Destination

  Software engineering is an interesting field to me because it combines problem solving and creativity to create products and technologies that benefit people. Through my previous and current coursework, I have slowly developed more love for the problems I face and content I learn. Learning different programming languages, data structures, and algorithms has taught me the critical thinking skills to approach various problems and organizational skills to solve that problem effectively and efficiently. 

  One area of software engineering that I would want to improve on is my ability to work with a team to design larger, more sophisticated programs. Going beyond just writing code, working and communicating with a team is a substantial part of being a software engineer. Developing collaboration skills through version control, documentation, and testing is a must for this career. Having a team also functions as an important proofreader for your projects by increasing its maintainability and reliability. I want to be able to provide the best work for my team and write code that is easy for them to understand. 
  
  Furthermore, the technical aspects of designing classes and interfaces and implementing data structures become more complicated and nuanced as the scale of the project grows. Gaining more experience in building projects with multiple components is something I also want to work on. The skills of designing complex, multi-faceted programs involves multiple rounds of debugging, testing, and revising of code.

  In the future, I hope to apply these skills to projects that allow me to build practical, impactful software and continue exploring different areas of technology, especially in the field of healthcare. My main goal is to become a more confident and well-versed software engineer who can take a problem, break it into smaller parts, design an effective solution, and implement and test that solution. I know that becoming a strong software engineer will require continued practice and learning, but the challenges I have encountered in my programming courses have made me interested in continuing to develop these skills.

-Ian Kubo
